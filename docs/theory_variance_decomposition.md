# Theoretical Foundation & Variance Decomposition

## Law of Total Variance

The central theoretical result explains why MCMC (full Bayesian sampling) and Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE plug-in) yield statistically equivalent predictive forecast distributions for customer purchase counts $x^*$.

By the law of total variance, the forecast predictive variance per customer $i$ decomposes into:

$$\mathrm{Var}(x^*_i \mid \text{data}) = \mathbb{E}_{\theta \mid \text{data}}\!\left[\mathrm{Var}(x^*_i \mid \theta, \text{data})\right] + \mathrm{Var}_{\theta \mid \text{data}}\!\left(\mathbb{E}[x^*_i \mid \theta, \text{data}]\right)$$

where $\theta = (r, \alpha, s, \beta)$ denotes the population heterogeneity parameters.

### Variance Order Analysis

1. **Individual Stochasticity ($\mathcal{O}(1)$)**:
   The first term represents individual-level stochasticity—the posterior uncertainty in $(\lambda_i, \mu_i, \tau_i)$ given customer $i$'s history $(x_i, t_{x,i}, T_i)$, compounded with Poisson count noise. This term is $\mathcal{O}(1)$ and does not shrink as the cohort size $N$ grows.

2. **Population Parameter Uncertainty ($\mathcal{O}(1/N)$)**:
   The second term represents population parameter uncertainty $\mathrm{Var}(\theta \mid \text{data})$. MCMC propagates this uncertainty while MLE holds $\theta$ fixed at $\hat{\theta}_{\text{MLE}}$. By standard asymptotic normality of posterior distributions, this term contracts as $\mathcal{O}(1/N)$.

For any realistic cohort size ($N \ge 100$), the individual-level stochasticity completely dominates population parameter uncertainty. Consequently, the predictive distributions generated by MCMC and MLE coincide.

## Proper Scoring Rules & Calibration

- **Continuous Ranked Probability Score (CRPS)**:
  $$\mathrm{CRPS}(F_i, y_i) = \mathbb{E}|X - y_i| - \frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}|X - X'|$$
  Rewards calibration and sharpness jointly.

- **Randomized Probability Integral Transform (PIT)**:
  For discrete count forecasts $Y$, the randomized PIT is defined as:
  $$u_i = F_i(y_i - 1) + v_i [F_i(y_i) - F_i(y_i - 1)], \quad v_i \sim U(0,1)$$
  Under calibration, $u_i \sim U(0,1)$. Departures are measured via Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance (PIT-KS).
